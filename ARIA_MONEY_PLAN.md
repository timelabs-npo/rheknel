## 1. Money reality

ARIA Track 2 projects are currently advertised at **£200k–£2m per project**.

ARIA can fund **100% of eligible project costs**. This is project funding, not unrestricted personal cash.

ARIA's standard payment model is reimbursement of actual eligible expenditure **in arrears**, usually quarterly, with monthly claims potentially agreed to support cash flow.

Therefore the application must solve two different problems:

1. win a large award;
2. make the award operationally financeable between spending and reimbursement.

The second problem is part of project design, not an afterthought.

## 4. Cash-flow design

Because reimbursement is in arrears:

- request monthly reimbursement if selected, rather than default quarterly, where possible;
- minimise personally fronted project costs;
- prefer subcontractor/payment terms aligned with ARIA claim cycles;
- if a lead organisation or UK partner is added, explicitly evaluate whether it can carry working capital;
- do not promise hires/equipment whose cash timing cannot be sustained;
- budget the required independent accountant/audit work.

A £900k award is useless if the project dies because the first reimbursement arrives after the first large payroll/invoice cycle.

## 5. What ARIA will pay for

Current eligible-cost guidance includes, when necessary and justified:
- labour;
- contractual employee benefits;
- materials;
- travel/subsistence;
- subcontractors/collaborators;
- equipment/facility use;
- approved other direct costs;
- approved indirect costs/overheads.

No grant profit margin should be assumed.

## 7. Money-maximising strategy without scope suicide

The objective is not to maximise the requested number.
It is to maximise:

```text
P(award) × usable award size × probability of delivery
```

A £2m proposal with weak differentiation is worth zero.
A £900k proposal with a concrete Background-IP baseline, a falsifiable new technical gap, measurable Arena metrics and a credible team/cashflow plan is financially superior.
